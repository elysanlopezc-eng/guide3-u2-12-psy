Core Concept 1

Experimental Method

A research method where the investigator manipulates one or more independent variables to observe their effect on dependent variables while controlling extraneous factors.

Causal Rigor 98%
Core Concept 2

Control & Experimental Groups

Experimental Group: Receives the active treatment/manipulation.
Control Group: Serves as a baseline comparison without treatment.

Baseline Comparison 94%
Core Concept 3

Random Assignment

Using chance procedures (e.g., coin flips, random number generators) to assign participants to experimental or control groups, minimizing preexisting differences.

Bias Reduction 95%
Core Concept 4

Cause-and-Effect Claims

The unique ability of true experiments to establish causality (A causes B) by eliminating confounding variables and establishing temporal precedence.

Internal Validity 96%
Core Concept 5

Strengths & Limitations of Experiments

Strengths: High control over variables, clear causal conclusions, replicability.
Limitations: Artificial lab environments may lack external validity / real-world naturalness.
Methodological Balance 91%
